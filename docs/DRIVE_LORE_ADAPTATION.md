## 3. Story spine — adapted, and where it is LOCKED

Spine order matches the lived hour: **Title → yard Tend → House → book → places → peoples**. Lore never arrives before hands (`TAPE_TRUTH` §2).

### 3.1 Five practices, after the House

Five and only five, and **after House** — founded or skip-named. Never a Title picker, never a boot lobby, never a stat.

**Human · Draek · Cydruid · Quellorian · Ambrosian.**

Canon spelling stays `GDD_ADAPTATION`'s name map (Druid → **Cydruid**, Quelorian → **Quellorian**, Draexx → **Draek**). Enslaved minion species stay narrative and non-playable. There is **no sixth people**, and no ninth (§10).

### 3.2 Drenadore

**The name is kept. The geography is not invented.**

Drenadore is the Drive pile's proper noun for the **seat of the hunger** — the Draek fall's own address, the place the cloning-era ethics collapse was administered from. In this tree it is a **codex proper noun after the book**: a word a stranger may read in a lore entry, hear in late speech, and never stand in.

- Not a Place. Four rooms stay four (`OFFLINE_SKU`).
- Not a hex, not a save-slot, not a travel row, not a fifth plate.
- Not a raid, not a siege, not a war week.
- Any physical detail beyond the name — layout, scale, fleet count, who sits there — waits on a **named steward line**. Bots invent no map.

### 3.3 Draek hunger — **LOCKED clinical**

The Drive pile's most abusable image is the feeding. It is locked, and the lock is not a rating decision — it is the moral read.

**Never eaten on screen. Never eaten off screen either.** There is no feeding beat, no gore cut, no implied chew in copy, no "and then they were consumed" line, no sound design for it, no VFX for it.

The pipeline is **clinical and industrial**, in this order and no other:

1. **Incapacitate.** Subject is put down, not killed for spectacle.
2. **Ship or portal.** Moved by hull or by portal. Not dragged, not devoured in place.
3. **Harvest bays.** Processing is a facility, a manifest and a tonnage — the horror is the *paperwork*, the bay lighting, the routing, the fact that it is a supply chain.

The Draek atrocity reads as logistics performed on people. That is worse than a mouth, it matches the Great Betrayal's "existence justifies all means," and it is the only register allowed. Harvest bays are **lore dress, never a playable Place, never a verb, never a Take node**.

### 3.4 Earth op — **LOCKED**

| Locked beat | Reading |
|---|---|
| **Nukes off** | The arsenals are neutralised before the op opens. No warhead fires, on screen or in codex. The Earth op is not a nuclear war story; disarming is a precondition, not a set piece. |
| **False-flag WWIII** | The cover is a staged human world war — humanity is manoeuvred into fighting itself so the extraction reads as ordinary catastrophe. The horror is the deception and the complicity, not the battlefield. |
| **Portal edition beats teleport beam** | Mass movement is by **portal**. The teleport beam / tractor lift is refused as the image and as the mechanism — it is the cheap sci-fi shot and it turns people into pixels. A portal is a door someone *walked through*, which keeps the atrocity legible. |

The Earth op is the Drive pile's spine at its strongest and it is **codex + late speech**, not an Hour-1 cutscene, not a Title backdrop, not a trailer promise.

### 3.5 Quellorian late mercy

Mercy arrives **late** and it is not a rescue.

The Quellorians carry the Great Betrayal as an old wound and move on Earth as unfinished business (`DRAEK_ORIGIN_AND_THE_GREAT_BETRAYAL` §6). Adapted: they are **not** an early cavalry, **not** a deus ex, and **not** a power spike handed to a player. Their mercy lands after the cost is already visible, it is chosen rather than owed, and it costs them. The Ambrosian late turn stays the same shape — reluctance is a calculus, not cowardice, and it breaks late.

**Refused:** an Hour-1 Quellorian ally NPC, a "they save you" opening, a mercy that reads as a buff.
